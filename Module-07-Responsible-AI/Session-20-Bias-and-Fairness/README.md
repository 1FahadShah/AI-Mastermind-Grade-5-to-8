# Session 20 - Bias & Fairness

> **Track A (Grades 5-8)** | **Module 7** | **Session 20 of 25**

---

## Overview

Session 20 introduces bias and fairness in AI.

The session explores how AI systems can work differently for different groups of people and examines how bias can enter an AI system through data, algorithms, and human decisions.

Learners investigate real-world cases and use an AI audit activity to think about how AI systems can be evaluated and improved.

---

## Purpose

The session is designed to help learners:

- Understand what bias means in AI
- Understand the idea of fairness in AI systems
- Identify possible sources of AI bias
- Explore real-world examples of biased AI systems
- Understand why different groups can experience different AI outcomes
- Learn how AI systems can be audited
- Consider ways to improve fairness in AI

## Session Construction

The session progresses through the following sequence:

**Warm-Up → Define → Identify → Explore → Analyze → Audit → Improve → Reflect**

### Warm-Up

Learners consider situations where a system might work better for some people than others.

This creates a starting point for discussing fairness in AI.

### Define

Bias is introduced as a situation where an AI system works better for some people than others.

Fairness is explored in relation to how AI systems affect different groups.

### Identify

Learners explore possible sources of bias in AI, including:

- Data
- Algorithms
- Human decisions

### Explore

The session examines real-world cases involving AI bias, including:

- Gender Shades (2018)
- Amazon hiring AI (2018)
- COMPAS (2016)

These cases provide examples for discussing how AI systems can produce different or problematic outcomes.

### Analyze

Learners examine how bias can enter an AI system and how different groups may experience different results.

### Audit

Learners participate in an AI audit activity to investigate an AI case and consider questions related to fairness.

### Improve

The session introduces ways AI systems can be improved, including:

- Improving data
- Testing different groups
- Transparency
- Accountability

### Reflect

Learners consider why fairness matters when AI systems are used and what people can do to identify and address bias.

## Examples & Applications

The session uses real-world cases to demonstrate how bias can appear in AI systems.

Examples include:

- **Gender Shades (2018)** - differences in facial recognition performance across groups
- **Amazon hiring AI (2018)** - concerns around bias in an AI-based hiring system
- **COMPAS (2016)** - a widely discussed case involving algorithmic decision-making and fairness

Learners use these cases to consider where bias can come from and how AI systems can be evaluated.

---

## Hands-On Component

**Junior AI Ethicist Activity**

Learners investigate an AI case and conduct a simplified AI audit.

The activity asks learners to:

1. Understand the AI system
2. Identify who is affected
3. Look for differences in outcomes
4. Consider possible sources of bias
5. Think about fairness
6. Suggest ways the system could be improved

The activity uses NotebookLM to support exploration of the selected case.

---

## Reflection

- What AI bias means
- Where bias can enter an AI system
- Why fairness matters
- How different groups can experience different AI outcomes
- How AI systems can be audited
- What could be done to improve a biased system

## Assessment & Extension

The session includes real-world case analysis, bias identification, AI auditing, discussion, and reflection.

The Junior AI Ethicist activity gives learners an opportunity to apply the concepts of bias and fairness to a specific AI case and think about possible improvements.
