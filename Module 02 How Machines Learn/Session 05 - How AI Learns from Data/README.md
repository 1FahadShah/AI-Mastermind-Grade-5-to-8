# Session 05 - How AI Learns from Data

> **Track A (Grades 5-8)** | **Module 2** | **Session 5 of 25**

---

## Overview

Session 05 focuses on the role of data in Machine Learning.

The session introduces datasets, features, training data, testing data,
generalization, overfitting, limited data, and bias. Learners examine why
the quality and variety of data matter when an AI system is learning.

The session prepares learners for Session 06, where they train and test an
AI model using a real dataset.

---

## Purpose

The session is designed to help learners:

- Explain what a dataset is and why it matters
- Explain what features are
- Distinguish between training data and testing data
- Understand why AI needs to learn patterns rather than memorize examples
- Explain generalization
- Recognize overfitting
- Identify problems caused by limited or biased data
- Understand why data quality matters

## Session Construction

The session progresses through the following sequence:

**Recap → Introduce → Explain → Compare → Analyze → Plan → Reflect → Extend**

### Recap

The session begins by revisiting the three types of Machine Learning from
Session 04 and connecting them to the role of data.

### Introduce

The session introduces the idea of **Garbage In → Garbage Out** and focuses
on data as a key part of the AI learning process.

### Explain

Learners are introduced to datasets and features using familiar examples.

A dataset is presented as a collection of examples, while features are
described as the clues AI pays attention to when making decisions.

### Compare

Training data and testing data are compared using the idea of practice
problems versus a real test.

The session then compares memorization with learning and introduces
generalization and overfitting.

### Analyze

Learners examine examples of overfitting, limited data, and biased data.

They also explore how a model can fail when its training data does not contain
enough variety.

### Plan

Learners plan data for a hypothetical healthy/unhealthy food AI by considering
examples, features, variety, and fairness.

### Reflect

Learners examine how poor data, irrelevant features, limited examples, or
lack of variety could affect an AI system.

### Extend

Homework prepares learners for the Cats vs Dogs model they will build in
Session 06 by planning features, data variety, and potential problems.

## Examples & Analogies

The session uses familiar comparisons to explain data and model behavior.

Examples include:

- Datasets as collections of practice examples
- Features as clues used by an AI system
- Training data as practice problems
- Testing data as new problems used to check whether the AI learned
- Memorization vs. learning as a comparison between remembering exact answers
  and understanding patterns

Cats and dogs are used repeatedly to demonstrate useful features,
generalization, and overfitting.

---

## Hands-On Component

The session includes a planning activity for a hypothetical
**Healthy / Unhealthy Food AI**.

Learners consider:

1. What examples the AI should learn from
2. What features the AI should use
3. How to include foods from different cultures and cuisines
4. How the data could become biased
5. How limited or unsuitable data could cause problems

---

## Reflection

Learners examine whether an AI is learning useful patterns or memorizing
details that do not matter.

They also consider how changes in data, such as lighting, background,
variety, or missing examples, can affect an AI system.

---

## Assessment & Extension

The session includes recap activities, concept exercises, scenario analysis,
data and feature activities, discussion, and the Healthy / Unhealthy Food AI
planning activity.

Homework prepares learners for Session 06 by asking them to:

- Identify features for a Cats vs Dogs classifier
- Consider what could confuse the AI
- Plan variety across breeds, colors, backgrounds, lighting, and angles
- Identify potential problems such as limited data, bias, overfitting, and
  unsuitable features
