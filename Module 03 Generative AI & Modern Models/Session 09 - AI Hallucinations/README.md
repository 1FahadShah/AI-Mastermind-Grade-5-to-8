# Session 09 - AI Hallucinations

> **Track A (Grades 5-8)** | **Module 3** | **Session 9 of 25**

---

## Overview

Session 09 examines AI hallucinations - situations where AI generates false,
fabricated, or nonsensical information while presenting it confidently.

The session builds on the Large Language Model concepts from Session 08 and
focuses on understanding why hallucinations happen, how to recognize them,
and how to reduce the risk of relying on incorrect AI output.

---

## Purpose

The session is designed to help learners:

- Define an AI hallucination
- Distinguish confidence from accuracy
- Understand why AI hallucinations happen
- Recognize different types of AI hallucinations
- Identify warning signs in AI-generated responses
- Apply strategies for reducing hallucinations
- Understand real-world consequences of incorrect AI output

## Session Construction

The session progresses through the following sequence:

**Recap → Connect → Explain → Compare → Investigate → Identify → Reduce → Extend**

### Recap

The session revisits concepts from Session 08, including language models,
tokens, attention, parameters, context windows, and the idea that LLMs
generate language through pattern-based prediction.

### Connect

A warm-up uses familiar situations where a person can be completely confident
about something and still be wrong.

This introduces the central idea of the session - AI can also sound confident
while providing incorrect information.

### Explain

An AI hallucination is introduced as AI-generated information that is false,
fabricated, or nonsensical but presented as if it were true.

### Compare

The session separates **confidence** from **accuracy**.

Learners examine different combinations of confidence and correctness,
including the particularly important case of high confidence with incorrect
information.

### Investigate

Real-world examples are introduced, including a lawyer who submitted
fabricated legal cases generated by ChatGPT and an airline chatbot that gave
incorrect policy information.

### Identify

Learners are introduced to different types of hallucinations and warning
signs that can help them recognize potentially unreliable AI output.

### Reduce

The session introduces ways to reduce hallucinations, including providing
context, being specific, asking for sources, and verifying AI output.

### Extend

Homework asks learners to compare answers from ChatGPT with information from a
reliable source using NotebookLM and document what they discover.

## Examples & Explanations

The session uses familiar examples to demonstrate the difference between
confidence and correctness.

It also examines real-world cases involving fabricated legal cases and
incorrect airline chatbot information to show that hallucinations can have
consequences beyond simple factual mistakes.

---

## Hands-On Component

**Tools:** ChatGPT and NotebookLM

Learners:

1. Choose a topic
2. Ask ChatGPT three specific factual questions
3. Find a reliable source
4. Provide the source to NotebookLM
5. Ask the same questions
6. Compare the responses
7. Document any differences or unsupported information

The activity gives learners direct practice in checking AI-generated
information against a source.

---

## Reflection

Learners consider:

- Whether an AI response can sound correct while being false
- Why confidence does not guarantee accuracy
- How hallucinations can be identified
- Why verification matters
- What responsibility a person has when using AI-generated information
