# Session 10 - Strengths & Limits of Generative AI

> **Track A (Grades 5-8)** | **Module 3** | **Session 10 of 25**

---

## Overview

Session 10 steps back from how Generative AI works and examines what it is
generally good at, where it struggles, and when it should or should not be
used.

The session brings together ideas from the previous Generative AI sessions
and introduces the **Human-in-the-Loop** approach, where AI assists while the
human decides, reviews, and remains responsible.

---

## Purpose

The session is designed to help learners:

- Identify common strengths of Generative AI
- Identify common limitations of Generative AI
- Decide when to use AI and when not to use it
- Understand the Human-in-the-Loop idea
- Analyze the strengths and limitations of a Generative AI tool

## Session Construction

The session progresses through the following sequence:

**Recap → Connect → Identify Strengths → Examine Limits → Decide → Analyze → Reflect → Extend**

### Recap

The session begins with the previous session's Hallucination Hunt and a quick
review of hallucinations, confidence vs. accuracy, and the role of NotebookLM.

### Connect

A super-powered hammer analogy introduces the idea that a powerful tool is
not necessarily the right tool for every job.

### Identify Strengths

Learners examine common strengths of Generative AI, including:

- Speed
- Brainstorming
- Drafting
- Explaining
- Languages
- Creating

Examples show how AI can help generate ideas, re-explain difficult concepts,
create drafts, translate, and produce content.

### Examine Limits

Learners examine common limitations including:

- No real understanding
- Hallucinations
- Outdated or missing information
- Bias
- No common sense
- Difficulty distinguishing true from false information

### Decide

Learners consider situations where AI can be useful and situations where it
should be avoided, including facts that matter, health and safety, personal
information, and important decisions.

### Analyze

Learners complete their own analysis of a Generative AI tool by examining
its strengths, limitations, prompts, hallucination examples, appropriate uses,
and situations where they would avoid using it.

### Reflect

Learners share what they discovered and develop a personal rule for using AI.

### Extend

Homework asks learners to identify one situation where they could use AI
appropriately and one situation where they should avoid using it.

## Examples & Explanations

The session uses a super-powered hammer analogy to explain that a tool can be
powerful while still being unsuitable for certain tasks.

Examples of AI strengths include brainstorming, re-explaining concepts,
drafting, translation, and content creation.

Examples of limitations include hallucinations, outdated information, bias,
lack of common sense, and lack of real understanding.

---

## Hands-On Component

**Tools:** ChatGPT, Gemini, or NotebookLM

Learners conduct a **Generative AI Analysis** of one selected tool.

The analysis covers:

1. What the tool does well
2. What it struggles with
3. A prompt that worked well
4. A hallucination example
5. When they would use the tool
6. When they would avoid the tool
7. Their personal rule for using AI

---

## Reflection

Learners share:

- The tool's biggest strength
- The tool's biggest weakness
- What surprised them
- Their personal rule for using AI

The session reinforces the idea that AI can assist with tasks while the human
remains responsible for deciding, reviewing, and using the output.

---

## Assessment & Extension

The session includes recap questions, strength and limitation activities,
use-or-avoid scenarios, discussion, and the Generative AI Analysis activity.

Homework asks learners to identify one appropriate use of AI and one situation
where they should avoid using AI.
